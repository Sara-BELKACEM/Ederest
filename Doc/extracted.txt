1. Startup Overview
Ederest runs two products on one stack: an LMS/Academy web application delivering professional programmes
(notably SAP), and a Digital Adoption Platform delivered as a Chromium browser extension. The backend is a
Python FastAPI modular monolith on Azure, with a React SPA front-end, PostgreSQL and self-hosted PostHog. AI is
already used in production to turn product screenshots into step-by-step tutorials.
2. Hackathon Challenge
Ederest has identified learner data collection as a priority need, starting where learners struggle most: learning to
use a business software. The platform captures technical events, but it does not systematically collect where a
learner gets stuck on a task, what they think at that moment, and why they give up. That feedback exists only in
scattered conversations, and today’s exercises assume a mouse, which leaves out learners who cannot use one.
The challenge is to build a lightweight, accessible feedback loop around a guided software task: detect the
moment a learner is stuck, offer help and a short question at that moment, then turn the raw free-text answers
into readable insight for the team.
3. Learning Science Frame
Every team leaves this hackathon with one testable product hypothesis. Before writing code, this team must be
able to answer the two questions below, and must revisit them at each daily checkpoint. A working prototype that
cannot answer them has not completed the challenge.
Product decision
Learning problem / learner behaviour: Learners get stuck on a step of a software task and abandon it, and the
team cannot see which step or why. Learners who cannot use a mouse cannot complete the task at all. The
learner behaviour we want to improve is persistence past the blocking step, but first we must be able to locate
that step.
Product decision that addresses it: Detect the stuck moment from the learner’s behaviour on the step (no
progress, repeated wrong clicks, leaving the task), offer an accessible hint and a one-tap question at that exact
moment, and compress the free-text answers into a ranked list of blockers the team can act on.
Learning mechanism supported: Just-in-time support at the point of difficulty: the hint helps the learner get past
the step without leaving the task. The feedback loop then acts on the content team: its value comes from whether
the blockers it surfaces are the real ones, and whether fixing them changes completion.
Evidence of learning
What the team must observe or measure to know whether the decision actually improved learning:

5. Recommended Team Composition
Team size: 4 participants
The Full-Stack Developers build the practice screen with its step guide, the embeddable micro-survey component,
the collection API and the insight dashboard. The AI Engineer builds the analysis layer that clusters free-text
answers into themes with sentiment and suggested actions. The Digital Marketer designs the stuck moments and
the survey wording so that response rates are high, defines the KPIs the dashboard must display, and recruits the
testers. Every participant contributes two idea cards (see section 7).
6. Proposed MVP
An accessible learner feedback loop made of four connected pieces: a guided practice task on a mock business
software screen, where a stuck learner is detected and offered a hint; a small survey widget that appears at
defined moments (stuck on a step, leaving the task, end of the task); a collection service that stores answers and
session events with the step context; and a dashboard where the Ederest team sees response rates, drop-off and
satisfaction per step, and an AI-generated summary of what learners struggle with and what should be fixed first.
The guide and the survey work with the keyboard only.

AI: an existing LLM API for clustering and summarisation. No model training.

Data: seed the database with 30-60 realistic sample responses and matching session events so the dashboard

12. Expected Final Deliverables
Required MVP deliverable
A working prototype where a judge practises the task, gets stuck on a step, answers the widget, then sees the
answer appear in the dashboard and be included in the AI-generated theme summary, with the keyboard only if
they choose.
Required evidence deliverable
The evidence report described in D3.2, presenting the measure defined on Day 1 and what it showed, including a
negative or inconclusive result, which the jury treats as a legitimate outcome when the measurement was honest.
Required ideas deliverable
Two idea cards per participant, in the handover pack.
Optional enhancements
Voice command, read-aloud, hints on/off test, sentiment view, drop-off analysis, export, multi-language handling.
13. Acceptance Criteria

•

•
No personal identifier is stored anywhere in the database.
•
The whole task, the hint and the survey can be completed with the keyboard only.

The whole loop can be demonstrated in under three minutes.
